# IMRAD Writing Guide

Use this module when drafting or revising a medical manuscript from analysis outputs.

## Title

Prefer titles that identify:

- Population.
- Exposure/intervention/index test.
- Outcome.
- Study design.

Avoid claiming causality in the title unless the design supports it.

## Abstract

Keep the abstract numerically consistent with the manuscript tables.

For structured abstracts include:

- Background/Objectives.
- Methods: design, setting, participants, exposure/intervention, outcomes, analysis.
- Results: cohort size, follow-up, primary effect estimate with CI, key secondary findings.
- Conclusions: proportionate interpretation.

## Introduction

Use a three-paragraph structure:

1. Burden and clinical importance.
2. Evidence gap and limitations of prior work.
3. Rationale and objective/hypothesis.

Every major claim needs a verified citation from the citation workflow.

## Methods

Include:

- Study design and setting.
- Participants and eligibility.
- Exposure/intervention/index test.
- Outcomes and measurement.
- Bias/confounding controls.
- Missing-data handling.
- Statistical analysis with model families, covariates, diagnostics, software, and sensitivity analyses.
- Ethics/IRB and registration when applicable.

## Results

Order:

1. Participant flow and exclusions.
2. Baseline characteristics.
3. Primary outcome.
4. Secondary outcomes.
5. Sensitivity/subgroup analyses.
6. Harms/adverse events when applicable.

Keep interpretation out of Results.

## Discussion

Use a five-part structure:

1. Principal findings.
2. Comparison with prior literature.
3. Possible mechanisms or interpretation.
4. Strengths and limitations.
5. Clinical/research implications and conclusion.

Avoid overclaiming exploratory, underpowered, post hoc, or imprecise findings.
